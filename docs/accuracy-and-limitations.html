How Accurate Is CityMind?

By Peng Jing · Updated August 2026

CityMind is not a verified research database. It can organize a question and suggest a research structure, but its generated content may be incomplete, generic or inaccurate. Policies, statistics, cases and citations must be checked against original sources.

Can CityMind replace professional urban research?

No. It is designed to provide a structured starting point. Formal planning, policy or consulting work still requires original-source review, current official data, local context, field research and professional judgment.

Does CityMind use retrieval-augmented generation?

No. The current version does not connect to a local policy, report or academic database. It therefore cannot guarantee that a statement is based on the latest or most relevant source.

Can CityMind provide citations?

The model may produce citation-like text, but such output must not be treated as verified. The system prompt asks the model not to invent citations. Users should provide real source excerpts or add sources after independent verification.

What information needs special verification?

  • Policy titles, issuing departments, dates and clauses;
  • Population, economic, land-use and facility statistics;
  • Case-study names, implementation details and outcomes;
  • Professional standards, legal requirements and technical thresholds;
  • Statements using words such as “latest,” “best,” “first” or “most effective.”

Recommended verification workflow

  1. Separate generated hypotheses from factual claims.
  2. Build a source register with URL, publisher, date and access date.
  3. Prefer official statistics, policy publishers and primary research.
  4. Check whether the evidence applies to the selected area and time period.
  5. Revise the brief so every important judgment is linked to evidence.
CityMind should not be used as the sole basis for legal, financial, medical, engineering or other high-stakes decisions.
